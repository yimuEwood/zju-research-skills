# Discriminating experiment design

For each proposed experiment record:

- experiment ID and hypotheses compared;
- experimental unit and sampling unit;
- intervention/exposure and comparator;
- positive, negative, vehicle/sham, batch, and measurement controls as applicable;
- primary outcome, measurement method, timing, and uncertainty;
- randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, and exclusion rules;
- predicted pattern under every hypothesis;
- prospective decision rule, multiplicity plan, and inconclusive region;
- feasibility, safety, ethics, data-governance, and resource constraints;
- information gain and next action.

Prefer perturbations and orthogonal measurements that yield different predictions across hypotheses. Replication improves precision but does not by itself distinguish mechanisms. A rescue or reversal design is useful only when alternative pathways and off-target effects are addressed.
