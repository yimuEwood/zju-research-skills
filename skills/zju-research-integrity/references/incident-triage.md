# Incident triage

1. Address immediate participant, environmental, chemical, biological, or data-security danger through authorized emergency/safety channels.
2. Preserve originals, logs, versions, hashes, access records, and the chain of custody. Make working copies; do not “clean” evidence.
3. Write a neutral chronology that separates observed facts, reported statements, anomalies, and interpretations.
4. Limit access to people with a legitimate role. Avoid public accusations, retaliation, coercive interviews, and unauthorized account/device access.
5. Identify the current official institutional process and decision authority. Verify contacts and policy text live before use.
6. Document what was referred, when, by whom, under what authority, and what evidence was preserved.

AI assistance can organize materials and highlight inconsistencies. It must not decide intent or misconduct, interview parties, or replace institutional fact-finding.
